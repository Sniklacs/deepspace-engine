## Q1 — Territory-war events: Last War: Survival & the genre

1. **Last War: Survival (Century Games) — closest analog.** City-builder + auto-battle horde campaign that pivots the server into recurring server-vs-server war after the story campaign (several weeks in). Top-grossing US mobile strategy stretches of 2024–2025, multi-billion cumulative revenue [verify vendor charts]. Known tension: endgame war transition + server power gaps dominate r/LastWarMobile complaint threads.
2. **Century/4X-zombie cluster anatomy (Last War, Whiteout Survival, State of Survival, Last Shelter):** recurring cross-server event = registration/prep → power-based server matching → campaign map with capture points (central castle/outposts/flags) → score ticks over a window (often hours with a "big battle" peak) → ranked rewards → repeat. Whiteout's flagship: **Sunfire Castle**. Tight peak windows = watchable; all-day ticking = tedious.
3. **Evony: The King's Return — weekly SvS** (Battle of Gaugamela): servers matched by total power, weekly Saturday battles, flags/keeps. Massive spend culture; among the most-cited **whale domination + weekly grind fatigue** examples.
4. **Rise of Kingdoms — KvK seasons (best-documented):** 2v2 kingdoms on a shared "Lost Kingdom" map, ~2-month campaigns, kills/objectives scoring, power-seeded matchmaking. Complaints: whale kingdoms roll F2P, top-alliance gatekeeping, all-or-nothing participation.
5. **Game of War (Machine Zone) — the whale era cautionary pole** and the genre shorthand for "money wins the war"; today's players react against it explicitly.
6. **Synthesis — compelling vs tedious:** compelling = one shared server-wide score, broadcastable climax windows, real reward stakes, collective identity, alliance politics. Tedious = 24/7 vigilance, night obligations, march/queue busywork, post-Day-1 steamrolls, score decided by few whales, participatory walls (zero score = zero reward; gatekeeping) that kill the middle class.

## Q2 — The "real-time" action layer

1. **Last War's loop:** heroes auto-battle zombie waves on a scrolling field while the player watches and occasionally uses skills; the base/city layer is classic timer-4X. The action layer is the *session hook* — spectacle between long timers (GameRefinery/Deconstructor of Fun analysis of the 4X+action hybrid wave).
2. **What players praise:** horde-mowing power fantasy, quick satisfying sessions, visible progression, low APM — auto-battle done well keeps agency small but feel big.
3. **What breaks it:** lag/desync in mass cross-server fights; realization it's a stat check with animations (strategy players leave); power walls turning "action" into pay-to-progress animation.
4. **Spectrum lesson (Clash Royale → Last War):** full manual real-time = skill expression + durable retention but excludes idle/management players; full auto = spectacle but no depth. **The winning position for our audience (colony-sim/4X) is "decision moments, not APM"** — a few consequential micro-decisions per fight (hero squad loadout, skill timing, purify/claim choice), everything else resolves with spectacle. Age of Empires Mobile shows active control's pull, but its audience isn't ours.
5. **Retention claim:** action-hybrid 4X sustains high D1/D7 by design; long-term story is war fatigue unless the war layer gives action lasting meaning — our persistent world (colonies/supply surviving offline) is structurally stronger than the genre's disposable war maps.

## Q3 — Week-long battleground / ladder formats

1. **Guild Wars 2 WvW — the best 7-day server-vs-server precedent:** 3 servers per weekly matchup (Monday reset), objective capture, continuous war score, tiered Glicko matchmaking, and the key fairness invention — **scoring in 2-hour "skirmish" buckets** so off-peak/night hours can't dominate (official wiki; ArenaNet World Restructuring posts). Night-capping debates remain. **The skirmish bucket is the single most borrowable fairness mechanic in the genre.**
2. **Clash of Clans CWL (7-day league):** 8 clans/group, one war daily for 7 days, league tiers + promotion/relegation, medals currency. "Why return daily" = fresh opponent + league stakes + daily schedule. Complaints: engineered matchmaking, top-roster-only participation — the 80% sitting out feel the ladder isn't for them.
3. **Clash Royale ladder:** monthly season, league floors (can't drop below), streak bonuses as comeback mechanics, seasonal resets. **Floors + streaks + visible rank feel fair even to losers**; pure top-score tables read as rich-get-richer.
4. **What kills participation mid-week (Eve/Foxhole/WAR evidence):** a war that *feels decided* (steamroll) and war fatigue (Eve's War Bee discourse; Foxhole wars dragging past decision; Eve's TiDi time-dilation cautionary tale).
5. **Cadence lesson (Foxhole):** the meta-ladder is "war number N" + history; wars that end cleanly and restart keep community returning; indefinite wars bleed players. **Comedown and truce days are retention technology, not dead time.**

## Q4 — Server-vs-server vs alliance-vs-alliance; server/race as identity

1. **Origin of RvR — DAoC / Warhammer Online:** 3 realms, keeps, and **relics whose capture grants realm-wide buffs** (server-level stakes). In both, the **server+realm WAS the identity** — and both suffered realm population imbalance that killed servers (merges/deaths). **Strongest precedent AND strongest warning for one-race-per-server.**
2. **WoW realm pride:** server-firsts, realm-known guilds, realm economies; the trauma of server merges/"connected realms" = identity loss felt deeply.
3. **Eve Online — how to manufacture real pride:** null-sec bloc identity, named wars (World War Bee), propaganda culture, and **verifiable community stats — zkillboard.com / evewho.com made every player's history public and checkable. Pride = narration + records.** Eve's identity unit is alliances, not servers — its wars have characters; ours needs the character layer (Emissaries).
4. **GW2 WvW → World Restructuring:** population optimization trumped server identity; community grief about losing server names is documented evidence server identity is real, valuable retention — but **brittle against population swings**.
5. **What creates REAL pride (not label):** (a) a name with history in a public record (killboards/war logs), (b) recognizable heroes + leaderboards (the Contribution Award!), (c) shared stories surfaced by the game (recaps, drama, comebacks), (d) visible symbols bound to the server alone, (e) actual consequences tied to the server (sealed worlds), (f) rituals (weekly reset, war day). Evony/Whiteout fail because their "server" is a matchmaking bucket number.

## Q5 — Asymmetric war mechanics (purify vs claim)

1. **New World influence system — best "win differently" precedent:** push Influence via missions until a threshold triggers war — pressure without ownership, zone states (yours/contested/incursion); the PvE Corruption Invasion is a direct structural cousin of our Chorus hunting ramp.
2. **DAoC relics — capture ≠ territory:** relics flip a realm-wide buff; a raid's goal is a whole-realm effect. Precedent for "purified zone = server-wide effect," not occupancy.
3. **Eve sovereignty/entosis:** sov capture is a disruption tug-of-war bar (link vs counter-link) — an influence meter pushed and pulled. Caution: players found entosis grindy; asymmetric tools must still be fun-minute-for-fun-minute.
4. **Aion regional state flipping:** fortress artifacts flip whole regions' alignment + faction-wide buffs — zone-state, not node-claims. With GW2's economic soft-war (dolyak/camp sabotage), you have the palette for "purify = zone-state + economic denial + buff."
5. **Foxhole — asymmetry via identity, not handicap:** different (not mirrored) factions; wars can be won logistically (severing supply). **Balance rule distilled:** the asymmetric side must (1) affect the *same shared score*, (2) have equal fun-per-minute, (3) be interruptible (counterplay), (4) cost real investment (never free), (5) never be purely defensive — purification must *win* things, not just deny.

## Q6 — Monetization inside territory war; what we must refuse

1. **What the genre sells during wars:** speed-ups, resource packs, stat buffs, hero shards/artifacts, repair/heal items, VIP battle perks, war passes, teleports, banners/city skins (some stat-bearing).
2. **Acceptance map:** cosmetics/emotes/banners with no stats = accepted and profitable (Fortnite/Riot); cosmetic-only paid event tracks = broadly accepted; **everything that changes war math = hated as p2w** (Evony/Game of War are the dirty words; "the biggest wallet's server wins"); **healing/repair monetization is the most loathed** ("defeat is a purchase prompt").
3. **Clean models:** (a) Clash of Clans — gems buy *time* only, everything else earnable; (b) Albion Online — no purchasable power, premium = access/convenience; (c) Eve PLEX (money→ISK) — rejected, our monetization doc already rules it out without a deep player market.
4. **"Earned relevance over bought power":** top-grossing titles also sell the ladder indirectly (passes, boosts); players tolerate it as long as *participation* is rewarded; backlash follows when the *outcome* is purchasable.
5. **Specific refusals for us (hard):** no stat boosts; no speed-ups during scored windows (a speed-up is a score advantage in an SvS ladder); no resource packs that feed armies; no repair/heal monetization; no hero power/shards; no stat-bearing cosmetics; no premium conversion into anything war-relevant (already locked in monetization.md §1.2–1.3, §6). Also refuse p2w-adjacent *structure*: power-only server matching, war passes whose free track is unplayable without paid, paying to extend battle windows.

## SO WHAT FOR DEEPSPACE — 8 recommendations for the Atlantis-style war layer

1. **"First Breach" scripted week for Oracle introduction (outcome-independent).** Oracles are met only after first cross-server battle ("you touched their soil") — so the first war between any two worlds opens with a scripted **First Breach event whose trigger is the first colony of each side standing on the other's territory — not the score.** Win or lose, every colony that breached meets its Oracle that first Sunday. Protects lore-critical contact from being a beginner-trap; "dominance opens the conversation" remains the post-first-contact gate.
2. **Weekly arc = purity turbine; truce day is a system, not downtime.** Days 1–2 "Gearing": war consumes expedition output (embers → war supply) — the PvE core loop *feeds* the war, no separate war economy. Days 3–5 "Campaign": ladder scoring with a rotating daily objective (hold keeps / purify zone / protect supply lanes / shield an ally). Day 6 "Climax": broadcastable 2-hour double-points golden window (announced 24h ahead). Day 7 "Truce": no scoring; Oracle/devotion stage (riddle day, cleansing, next-week focus, Emissary declarations). **War week raises Chorus attention server-wide; Truce day is when cleansing must happen — aggression and purity become the same turbine.**
3. **GW2 skirmish buckets + comeback multipliers for fairness.** Score in 2-hour buckets so every time zone weighs equally. Steamroll valve: trailing side (down >25%) gets elevated published multipliers on PvE-adjacent contributions (purifications, hauls, shields) — never purchasable, never on direct kills. Keeps the trailing side playing a different game that still feeds the ladder + Contribution score.
4. **Zone-state map — Corrupted/Contested/Purified — purify as a *win*, not a denial.** Purified zones (a) stop enemy score tick, (b) deny enemy resource extraction, (c) locally slow the Chorus hunting ramp. **Unbound worlds compete on the same ladder**: purification ticks + shield assists convert to score at published parity rates. Guardrails: purify costs expedition resources, is interruptible, 24h re-purify lockout, "purified zone cannot be re-corrupted for X hours" floor — purification *wins* score. **The purify tool is earned-only** — unlocks only for colonies/worlds that achieved Unbound (silent reveal) — nobody picks the easy asymmetric side.
5. **The War Council — Emissary layer made visible (the genre's missing stakes).** Cross-world channel = the handful of Oracle-trusted + Contribution-Awarded Emissaries. Ship a **War Council UI**: pre-war declarations, focus calls, truce proposals, after-action statements — appended to a server-side ledger. An Emissary's word binds the world's *honor*: broken truces cost Oracle favor like betrayal. Diplomacy that costs trust, not gold.
6. **Public war-stats + History Book for pride + Contribution Award.** Read APIs/exports (zkillboard/foxholestats-style) so fan sites and streamers render per-world progress, hero feats, purifies, battle records. Server maintains a **History Book** ("the Burn of Cycle 12") — recaps, firsts, comebacks. The weekly **Contribution Award** is computed from these same objective numbers. Can ship early on the beta world as a contribution score.
7. **Heroes as the war's unit layer with a server-side participation cap.** Squad rotation (3–5 heroes, L3 specializations) defines the war loadout; heroes earned deterministically, power never purchasable. Add a **per-week hero energy cap** bounding total marches per player per day — fairness valve against no-life grind AND whale stamina, a **cap, not a shop** (non-extendable by purchase). Participation yields Contribution score even in a losing week (answers CoC CWL gatekeeping).
8. **The explicit never-copy list (p2w + structural).** Never sell during scored windows: stat boosts, speed-ups/resources feeding armies, repair/heal items, hero power, stat cosmetics, power-bearing war passes, premium conversion. Structural never-copies: (a) no pure-power server matching — seed on activity+power composite; (b) no permanent war — recurring scheduled with Truce day + lore-diegetic opt-out floor (a race world below a minimum activity threshold "holds its border" and sits the week out — protects dead worlds from being steamrolled into uninstall); (c) no alliance-only participation; (d) no whale shortcuts to ladder position — ladder result not purchasable by design, every scored input is money-proof. **Flag:** the war-season battle pass's paid track must be *strictly cosmetic* (banner kits, sigil frames, Votives/Scrip-neutral trinkets); any "war convenience" in it (queue slots, extra marches) violates (a)–(d).

**Structural risk to flag for the owner:** one-race-per-server = six-way population split. GW2's restructuring and WAR/DAoC realm-death history say server identity is powerful *and* brittle. Mitigations: (i) war pairs rotate across the six worlds rather than fixed rivalries; (ii) activity floor (rec. 8b); (iii) one account/colony per world so quiet worlds degrade gracefully.
