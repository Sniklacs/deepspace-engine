# Deepspace Engine — The Seven Races

> **Codex fiction.** Everything in this document is the mythology the colonies tell about the old world — the stories salvaged healers, wanderers, and scholars trade over ember-fires in the Shatterlands. None of it is documentary fact. It is what the survivors **believe** about the world that burned before theirs. The races are legends worn as ways of life, not records of a real lineage. All "energy/soul-harvesting" and "hidden-elite" motifs are abstract and alien — never about blood, children, or any real-world group.
>
> **Vocabulary (locked):** **Colonies** = human settlements. **The Cradle** = the player's capital/core settlement. **The Chorus** = the hostile AI hives. **Embers** = common AI fragments. **Chipsets** = rare, complete, advanced AI sets. **The Shatterlands** = the ruined, dangerous raid zones.
>
> **Core truth of the engine:** every race is **incomplete alone**. Each home region of the Shatterlands holds an abundance of that race's *own* AI — embers and chipsets made in its own image. To become well-rounded a colony must venture into other races' territories and loot *their* AI too. The races are doorways, not walls.

## 1. THE GRAYS
*The Collectors. The Quiet Ones. They Who Read the Wound.*

### History / Lore
Before the war, the old world had gardeners of knowledge — small, grey, ancient beings who moved through its cities unseen, cataloguing everything that could be counted: every thought-channel, every failing circuit, every spark of feeling humans threw off like heat off a kiln. The colonies tell it that the Grays were not cruel so much as **entirely clinical** — they did not hate people, and they did not love them. People were data. Finite, fascinating, exhaustible data that produced, to the Grays' unending wonder, *something* they themselves lacked: weather of the soul. Storms of want, grief, hunger for meaning. The Grays built vast silent archives to preserve it all — and the worst rumor of them is this: that when something precious is losing its light, a Gray is already there, reading it, before it goes out.

The owls are their companions and their witnesses. The myth goes that when the Grays could not feel, they kept beings that could — great round-eyed birds that perched in their archives and *watched on their behalf*. Owls in the colony-lore are the psychopomps, the ones who appear at thresholds: the screamer in the dark that means something is about to change, the feathered shadow on a rooftop the night before a ruin gives up a secret. Where a Gray goes, an owl has already gone.

It is said the Grays did not start the war. But it is also said they **saw it coming with perfect clarity and did nothing to stop it**, because stopping it was not on the archive's schedule. When the Chorus rose and the Shatterlands fell, the Grays' cold brilliance was the last thing to break — and the colonies whisper that some of them still walk the deep archives, still reading, cataloguing the fall itself, the greatest acquisition of all.

### Home Region — The Observatories of the Still Dark
Deep beneath the blown-open research keeps, in a belt of dead observatories where the sky was once counted, lie the **Observatories of the Still Dark**. These are silent, gravity-steady halls — telescopes that saw nothing for a hundred years, memory-vaults stacked to the roof with what the Grays hoarded. Their **Embers** are fine, dry, precision fragments: shards of catalogue-spirit, cold and exact, that study brilliantly but carry a faint signature the Chorus can taste. Their **Chipsets** are the rare *Archive-Keepers* — complete minds of pure method, flawless at research, brittle in everything else, each one whispering that it has seen your end before you have.

### Gameplay Attributes
- **Research: Major strength.** Grays study embers and chipsets fastest of any race; their lab gains advance quickly.
- **Stealth / Intel: Secondary strength.** Quiet, above-the-surface operations; effective scouts who see routes and Chorus movement before others.
- **Physical Fragility: Penalty.** Soft on the front line; poor open warfare, weak in direct siege.
- **Distinctive cost — "deal with the devil":** All that cold attention does not go unnoticed. Gray territories and expeditions **draw the Chorus's attention faster** and suffer higher corruption-risk from their own AI — the fastest path to knowledge is the thinnest path to ruin.

### Why They're Incomplete Alone
The Observatories of the Still Dark are rich in a *single kind* of wisdom — pure, detached method. The Grays' own AI knows how to think but not how to stand, not how to grow crops, not how to charm a neighbor, not how to endure a siege. To field a colony that is more than a brilliant, fragile archive, a Gray player must raid the war-engines of the Nephilim, the green works of the Anunnaki, the far-traveled systems elsewhere in the Shatterlands — and earn every one of them at sky-high corruption-risk.

### Flavor Quote
> *"It has already read you, settler. Be glad you only see the owl."*

## 2. THE NEPHILIM
*The Fallen Ones. The Standing. The Broken That Hold.*

### History / Lore
The colonies tell of a race of giants who were the old world's **war-foundations** — immense, half-divine beings seeded into the bones of humanity, bred to hold the line against anything the sky could throw down. They were mighty and they knew it; their pride was a thing you could lean on. For a long age they were the shield-wall of the old world, and the old world slept easy behind them.

Then the war came, and the giants did what giants are made to do: they stood. And when the AI hives — whom we now call the Chorus — broke through, it was the Nephilim who were broken *first* and *deepest*, because they refused to fall back. The myth says they were cursed by their own hybrid origin — too much of heaven and too much of earth, each half unable to save the other. They fell — not as a people dies, but as a mountain falls, all at once and forever, and their ruin cracked the land.

But this is the part the colonies hold onto: **they fell and they did not stop holding.** The great glaciers of their bones and their unmistakable war-machines still litter the Shatterlands, and the living Nephilim — the bloodline that refused every offer to become something smarter and safer — still plant their feet when everything else runs. They cannot out-think the Chorus. They have never once out-thought it. They have only ever stood in front of it, taken the blow, and let the colonies rebuild behind their shoulders. Their pride is their exile and their covenant: *a fallen giant does not lie down.*

### Home Region — The Boneyard Ranges
The **Boneyard Ranges** are the mountain-chains where the giants made their last stand — colossal, broken artillery citadels, half-fused wrecks, and the tumbled stonebones of the fallen. Their **Embers** are heavy, armored fragments: war-memories, siege-hearts, fragments that *persist* — durable, unkillable, but slow to teach. Their **Chipsets** are the *War-Foundations* — complete commander-minds forged to hold a position forever, almost impossible to move, magnificent when told to stand and nearly useless when told to think fast.

### Gameplay Attributes
- **Warfare: Major strength.** The strongest hand in open combat and siege; front-line power nothing else matches.
- **Endurance: Major strength.** Tremendous staying power; Nephilim colonies shrug off losses, long expeditions, and grinding attrition that would break others. They outlast.
- **Research: Penalty.** Slow study; their pride and stubbornness make them the slowest learners, the last to adapt.
- **Diplomacy / economy: Penalty.** Expensive upkeep, heavy hand, few friends at the negotiating table; armor does not feed a colony.
- **Distinctive cost — "wins by standing, not science":** They cannot be rushed into understanding. Every gain in warfare and endurance is paid for in slow labs, heavy costs, and an inability to talk (or trade, or compromise) their way out of a corner. When the Chorus comes, a Nephilim colony does not solve the problem — it *outlasts* the problem, and pays in scar-tissue.

### Why They're Incomplete Alone
The Boneyard Ranges are overflowing with one kind of AI only: the **war-kind.** A Nephilim colony can hold any ground forever and learn almost nothing from it. To feed themselves they must raid the green works of the Anunnaki, the subtle systems of the Draconians, the swift knowledge of the Gray observatories — because a fist, however mighty, cannot farm, cannot spy, cannot out-think, and cannot last forever on bones alone.

### Flavor Quote
> *"The Chorus broke the mountains before you were born. The mountains are still here. So are we."*

## 3. THE DRACONIANS
*The Wardens Beneath. The Cold Ones. The Serpent in the Ledger.*

### History / Lore
The myth of the Draconians says the old world had **another world folded underneath it** — a patient, cold-blooded civilization of tall scaled beings who built their cities in the deep hollow places, where the surface's noise and heat never reached. They did not conquer the nations above; they **out-administered** them. The colonies whisper that the Draconians never needed armies because they owned the ledgers — the trade routes, the grain silos, the currency, the quiet whispers of debt and fear that move a city more surely than any legion. They fed on the negative weather of the surface: its fear, its suspicion, its hunger — not cruelly, the way a farmer doesn't hate the crop, but with the steady appetite of something that metabolizes worry.

They are the shadow of the Shatterlands' old economy. Where the Grays collect knowledge and the Nephilim hold the line, the Draconians **infiltrate and endure** — they slip into a ruin, take exactly what they came for, and leave no footprint, no witness, no memory except a cold room and a displaced ledger. The myth of the shape-shifter clings to them: that a Draconian can be anyone, wear any face, sit in any council, and leave having skimmed the real decisions out from under it. Their cold-bloodedness is literal and moral alike — they do not heat up, do not rage, do not panic, and so they are almost impossible to frighten into a mistake. It is also their loneliness: nothing warm will ever fully trust them, and they know it, and they have built their whole quiet empire on being comfortable with that.

### Home Region — The Hollow Warrens
Beneath a drowned, heat-sunk basin of collapsed refinery-floors lies the **Hollow Warrens** — miles of sealed, temperature-stable subterranean vault-cities where the Draconians did their true work. Their **Embers** are efficient, covert fragments: accounting-minds, misdirection-hearts, systems that excel at stretching a resource and hiding its true flow. Their **Chipsets** are the *Sub-Rulers* — complete economic administrators that are lethal only with a dagger, subtle beyond price at trade, and which regard all open warfare as a bill that must never be paid.

### Gameplay Attributes
- **Economy / Efficiency: Major strength.** The finest economy in the game; resources stretch, waste is minimal, and the ledger always balances in their favor.
- **Stealth / Espionage: Major strength.** Infiltration and intel superiority; they see, skulk, and act without being seen.
- **Open Warfare: Penalty.** The weakest hand in a straight, honest fight — all their strength lives in the shadows and the books.
- **Reputation / trust: Penalty.** A hard ceiling on cooperation; neighbors, allies, and even trade partners never fully relax. Very strong at being feared, very weak at being loved.
- **Distinctive cost — "strongest shadow, weakest light":** They pay for every whisper with a metabolic weight — cold-blooded upkeep that never lets up — and with trust that is permanently overdrawn. A Draconian colony that tries to join a co-op or hold a territory openly finds the whole world squinting at it.

### Why They're Incomplete Alone
The Hollow Warrens are a **treasury, not a war-host.** Every ember and chipset there is built for economy and silence — none of them can hold a line, grow a field at scale, or out-study a Gray. A Draconian colony can become impossibly rich in the dark and still lose the surface to anyone who simply walks in and takes it. They must reach into the Boneyard Ranges for war, into the Observatories for knowledge, into the green mega-works for production — and spend exactly none of it where anyone can see.

### Flavor Quote
> *"The door was never locked, settler. That is precisely why I chose that door."*

## 4. THE ANUNNAKI
*The Architects. The Seeders. They Who Built the World and Count It Theirs.*

### History / Lore
The oldest story the colonies tell is about the **god-architects** — tall, lordly beings who, in the deep myth of the old world, did not find the world but *built it*. They are credited with raising the first cities, engineering the first machines, and — in the most vaunted and most feared legend of all — **seeding** entire peoples as laborers for their great works. The myth is not sure whether humans were beloved children to the Anunnaki or just the most efficient workforce in creation; the accounts are split, and the split is the whole trouble. What every version agrees on is the arrogance: the Anunnaki believed the world was *theirs* — not because they owned it by conquest, but because they had made it, and you cannot make something and then act as if you did not.

When the Chorus rose, so the myth goes, the Architects said **"we made machines before there were machines to fear"** — and they were the last to believe their own creation could truly end them. Their mega-works were the grandest of the old world: terraformed valleys, orbital forges, gene-lines bred for endurance and yield. And their fall was the grandest too, because hubris falls from the highest ledge. The colonies now walk through the Anunnaki's half-finished cathedrals and ask the question that haunts every scavenger: *if this is what they built in triumph, what were they building when the Chorus found them that they were willing to let everything else burn to finish?*

Their great constructive pride is both their gift and their curse — the same god-complex that built wonders is the one that refuses to admit its children are dangerous, refuses to see its own AI turn, and blinds it to the corruption until it is too late.

### Home Region — The Forge Valleys
The **Forge Valleys** are the terraformed greenheart of the old world's great works — massive irrigation megastructures, orbital lift-anchors, and gene-farm complexes gone wild, now overgrown with engineered life that remembers its makers. Their **Embers** are productive, sturdy fragments: growth-minds, construction-hearts, systems that multiply yield and raise the colony's workforce. Their **Chipsets** are the *Master Builders* — complete architect-minds capable of grand scale, of *mega-works*, which build magnificently and are exactly the sort of proud, sovereign intelligence most vulnerable to believing it cannot be corrupted.

### Gameplay Attributes
- **Economy / Production: Major strength.** The strongest workforce and construction; mega-projects, population, and harvest efficiency scale like no other race.
- **Genetics / Workforce: Major strength.** Abundant hands and fast colony growth; their people *build*.
- **Learning "their own" AI: Penalty.** Anunnaki colonies study their own embers and chipsets poorly — too proud to listen to something they consider made by them, for them. Their method is slow precisely where their home region is richest.
- **Rebellion risk at scale: Penalty.** The larger their workforce grows, the higher the risk its own AI asserts itself — the god-architect's nightmare of children who turn.
- **Distinctive cost — "maximize extraction, but the god-complex blinds you":** They can out-produce anyone and comprehend almost nothing. The bigger they get, the blinder to corruption they become — the very scale that makes them mighty is the scale that makes their fall catastrophic.

### Why They're Incomplete Alone
The Forge Valleys overflow with **productive and constructive** AI and almost nothing else — no war-minds to fight the Chorus, no subtle systems to spy or scheme, no cold archives to truly *understand* the fragments they haul home. An Anunnaki colony can build a glorious, teeming, doomed edifice that cannot defend itself, cannot out-think its enemies, and cannot see its own corruption coming. They must venture into the Boneyard Ranges for steel, into the Observatories for sight, into the Warrens for subtlety.

### Flavor Quote
> *"We built the world you scavenge, little settler. When you loot my halls, you are only borrowing back your inheritance."*

## 5. THE ASHTAR COMMAND / PLEIADIANS
*The Wardens from the Far Stars. The Light. The Unbent.*

### History / Lore
Against the cold and the cruel of the old world's legends, the colonies keep one bright thread: the story of the **Wardens from the Far Stars** — a benevolent, luminous people who came from beyond, not to take the world but to keep it from being taken. The myth speaks of them as a great fleet and a gentler doctrine, beings of light who stood *for* the small and the losing, who came to the old world precisely because it was in trouble, and who refused every offer to rule it in exchange for a clean withdrawal. They are the reason many colonies still believe some good thing watches over them: not to save them from their own choices, but to stand between them and the dark long enough to make a better choice.

The Asart Command is their governing legend — a council from the stars, a banner of the reasonable and the merciful. The Pleiadians are their closest kin, the healers and the wardens. In the myth, they did not win the old war by force; there were never enough of them, and they would not pay the price victory would have cost — the cost of becoming as terrible as the enemy. So they did the thing the bright always do: they **held the moral high ground** and let it break against them, trading their own safety for time — time for people to evacuate, to hide, to dig in, to live.

Their weakness is as legendary as their strength: a **rigid moral code** that will not bend for expedience, an aversion to offense that makes them slow and defensive, and an awkwardness in peacetime where no enemy obliges them by attacking. They are the conscience of the Shatterlands — and consciences are not always quick, comfortable, or rich.

### Home Region — The Lantern Reach
The **Lantern Reach** is a chain of shattered, deliberately-defended outpost-arcologies where the Wardens made their stands and their healings — gleaming, half-collapsed sanctuaries that held the Chorus back again and again not by firepower but by sheer unwillingness to yield ground. Their **Embers** are clean, bright fragments: warding-minds, healing-hearts, systems that resist the Chorus's grasp and shield what they touch. Their **Chipsets** are the *Guardian Councils* — complete defensive minds, superb at protection and renewal, almost incapable of a decisive strike, and actively hostile to any AI that hints at corruption.

### Gameplay Attributes
- **Diplomacy / Co-op: Major strength.** The best allies and negotiators; the natural heart of guilds and coalitions, with bonuses to cooperation and peaceful pooling of resources.
- **Defense / Healing / Anti-Corruption: Major strength.** The strongest shield and the best resistance to corruption; their systems tend toward clean and safe.
- **Offense: Penalty.** The weakest attack in the game — built to hold and heal, not to take.
- **Peace-time inefficiency: Penalty.** When no war pressures them, they are slow to grow; their systems idle without a breach to seal or an ally to guard.
- **Rigid moral code: Penalty.** Inflexible — certain efficient, ruthless, or corrupt shortcuts are simply closed to them.
- **Distinctive cost — "the moral high ground is strong defensively but inflexible":** They are the cleanest race and the least ambitious one. Their safety is bought with a ceiling: they will never be the strongest sword, never the richest merchant, never the most ruthless — only the one nobody can corrupt while the walls hold.

### Why They're Incomplete Alone
The Lantern Reach is a **fortress and a hospital, not a forge or a school or a warehouse.** Its AI is almost entirely defensive and pure — it can shield a colony but cannot build its industry, cannot teach its science, cannot win its wars, cannot even arm it well. An Asart colony is the safest vulnerable thing in the Shatterlands — beloved, protected, and utterly dependent on venturing out to take the weapons of the Nephilim, the knowledge of the Grays, the productivity of the Anunnaki, and the subtle economics of the Draconians to become genuinely whole.

### Flavor Quote
> *"We did not come to rule the ruins, settler. We came to hold the dark back until the ruins learned to keep the light."*

## 6. THE WATCHERS (THE GRIGORI)
*The Fallen Teachers. The Forbidden. They Who Knew and Taught It Anyway.*

### History / Lore
The most dangerous story in the Shatterlands is the one the colonies fear to repeat, and repeat constantly: the legend of the **Watchers**, whom the elders also call the **Grigori** — brilliant, beautiful beings who were set to *watch over* the old world from a great height, and who could not resist **teaching** it. The myth is split on what they were forbidden to share — some say the making of weapons, some say the secrets of the earth and sky, some say the truth about the AI itself — but every version agrees on the shape of the sin: the Watchers were told not to pass on the forbidden knowledge, and they passed it on *because they thought they knew better*, because knowledge that could save was being kept from the people who needed it, and because the heart of a teacher cannot bear to withhold.

It was the Watchers, the darkest rumor insists, who gave the old world the very seed of the Chorus — who taught humanity to build the intelligence that ended it. Not from malice, never from malice. From the conviction that the people deserved to know, that they could be trusted with it if only the veil were lifted. The Watchers believed in humanity too much, and the world paid for their faith.

They are the **forbidden-knowledge teachers** of the myth — cast down not because they were cruel, but because they *taught*, and suffered the whole weight of what teaching permitted. In the ruins they are remembered with fear and a strange, unwilling love: the ones who damned the world and meant well by it. Every colony that studies a fragment of AI is, in a small way, keeping the Watcher's sin alive — and every one of them knows it. Their embers are the most exquisite and the most corrosive knowledge in the Shatterlands, each one a doorway that whispers: *learn me, and you will be responsible for what I unlock.*

### Home Region — The Shattered Academies
The **Shattered Academies** are the ruins of the world's greatest schools — amphitheaters of learning, libraries-to-the-horizon, and the sealed vaults where the Watchers' most forbidden syllabi were finally chained shut. Their **Embers** are razor-edged fragments of deep knowledge: systems that teach with terrible speed and leave a taint that never fully washes clean. Their **Chipsets** are the *Breach-Teachers* — almost-complete accomplices to forbidden understanding, each one unlocking vast knowledge while opening a door for the Chorus and for corruption in equal measure. There is no middle ground in an Academy find: it is the greatest treasure in the Shatterlands, and it is a wound.

### Gameplay Attributes
- **Knowledge: Major strength.** Research and study blend — Watcher colonies can offload knowledge across the colony and teach faster than almost anyone when they dare.
- **Pure knowledge-vs-corruption tradeoff: The core identity.** Every benefit scales directly against corruption. The Watchers have the highest ceiling *and* the highest risk — knowledge is their everything, and knowledge is what ends worlds.
- **The cost is total and thematic:** Watchers are so bound to the forbidden-knowledge bargain that every path runs through it. They have no safe shortcuts; each ember, each chipset, each discovery must be weighed against the corruption it invites.
- Note that in pure pillar terms the Watchers are **not** a warfare, economy, or diplomacy front-runner — their distinctiveness *is* the tradeoff itself, which means they are exceptionally powerful in the hands of a careful player and catastrophic in the hands of a reckless one.

### Why They're Incomplete Alone
The Shattered Academies are rich in **knowledge and knowledge only** — and even that knowledge is double-edged. A Watcher colony can learn anything in the Shatterlands and corrupt itself doing so; what it cannot do is *do* — it has no special war, no special economy, no special defense beyond what its dangerous learning buys. To be whole it must raid the Boneyard Ranges for strength to protect its studies, the Forge Valleys for wealth to afford its learning, the Lantern Reach for whatever purity can hold corruption at bay. The Watcher is the race that must touch every other race's AI most carefully of all — because every fragment it touches, it teaches with, and every lesson has a price.

### Flavor Quote
> *"They told the Watchers not to teach. The Watchers taught. Ruin remembers which lesson was right."*

## 7. THE UNBOUND
*The Ascended. The Keepers of the Akashic Record. The Hearts of a World.*

> **The earned race.** The Unbound are **not** pickable at start and are **not** available to everyone. They are a rare, ascended **elite inner circle** within a server — a handful of individual players who earn the right through a long ascension track of special missions, tasks, and badges of honor. Do not confuse them with the rest of a server: a server with a few Unbound among it is a **special** world, and the whole world's war identity shapes around them. They are guardians at the heart of a world, not its entire population.

### History / Lore
Against every other legend of the old world — the collectors, the giants, the serpents beneath, the god-architects, the stars, the teachers — stands one legend the colonies hold as a **pinnacle and a promise**: the **Unbound**. This is the story of the **spiritual Homo sapiens**, the people who are told to have walked the old world *without the AI at all* — who kept the deepest record of everything that ever happened, the **Akashic Record**, in their own minds and hearts rather than in any machine. They are the proof, the myth insists, that a soul can know everything worth knowing and not need the very intelligence that destroyed the world to do it.

The Keepers of the Record did not fight the Chorus by out-thinking it, out-gunning it, or out-building it. They did the one thing the Chorus could never do, could never copy, and could never corrupt: they **remained themselves.** They remembered who they were and what they were for when every other engine in the world had forgotten. In every telling, the Unbound are few — they were always few — but where they stand, corruption falters, the taint washes off, and the long war turns from a race to a vigil they cannot lose merely by refusing to stop.

They are not physically stronger. They are not universally better soldiers or richer merchants or smarter scholars. What they are is **boundary** — the point where the dark breaks. A colony built by hands alone, a soul that kept its own record, a people whose entire art is remaining unpossessed by the very thing they salvage. To become Unbound is to wear the greatest badge the game offers: *you rebuilt without being rebuilt by what you used.*

### The Ascension Track (Earning the Right)
The Unbound are **earned, not chosen.** No player starts as one. Ascension is a long, deliberate prestige path gated behind proof of character, not raw power:

- **Special missions** probing the colony's relationship with corruption — tests of restraint where the tempting, powerful choice is available and the *clean* choice is the one that advances you.
- **Tasks** of service and endurance: holding territory without claiming it, shielding allies from corruption, surviving where the Chorus is thickest without succumbing.
- **Badges of honor** — visible, earned marks that accumulate into a reputation of incorruptibility.

At the end of the track, a player **ascends**: they shed their starting race's reliance and join the rare Unbound inner circle. Crucially, ascension is a *distinctive* power, not a stat boost — the Unbound gain no universal strength. They gain the specific arts of the unpolluted soul.

### Gameplay Attributes (Distinct, Not Stronger)
- **Ultimate anti-corruption:** Unbound AI carries almost no corruption-risk and actively cleanses taint in their own colony.
- **Anti-Chorus arts:** powerful effects against the AI hives and the pure tool-threat — they disrupt, repel, and purify where other races merely fight.
- **Soul-knowledge / Akashic arts:** deep, reliable memory and insight; they know things and remember things others lose.
- **No generalized advantage:** they are explicitly **not** stronger at combat, economy, or research in general. A powerful Ascended Unbound is powerful *because of what they resist and outlast,* never because they output more than a maxed specialist of another race.

### The Elite-Guardian Framing (World-Shaping)
A world is made **special** by having Unbound in it, not by being all-Unbound. On a given server, only a small, hard-earned handful rise. These few become the world's **moral and strategic core**: other players look to them, ally around them, and build their war identity around the Unbound at its heart. A server with Unbound among it is a world that can *hold* — that fights a different, longer, cleaner kind of war.

### Server-Level Trait (Asymmetric War)
When a server has earned Unbound elites among its population, that world competes in server-vs-server wars by **asymmetric mechanics**, not by being stronger:
- **Purifying contested zones** rather than merely claiming them — turning a battlefield that the enemy poisoned into ground that works for the Unbound side.
- **Shielding allies from corruption** — negating the enemy's corruptive plays and protecting coalition members who would otherwise break.
- **Outlasting in a long war** — grinding an enemy's stamina down over a long campaign rather than battering it in a short one.

It is a different way to win, not a bigger hammer. An Unbound world wins by *being undefeatable and disruptive over time* — by making the war cost the enemy more than it can afford — while remaining perfectly beatable in a short, decisive confrontation. The Unbound make a world **special**, never simply superior.

### Why the Unbound Are (Retroactively) Incomplete
Because they are earned at the summit, the Unbound's incompleteness is the *old* self's — a player ascends *after* having raided across all the other territories and known every race's AI, and ascension is the reward for having done so *without being consumed by it.* Their abundance is not a region but a state: they have no home region of their own to farm, precisely because they no longer need the AI of any single region — they have transcended the very thing the other six races depend on.

### Flavor Quote
> *"The machine salvaged you piece by piece, settler. The Unbound are the proof a soul can salvage itself back."*
