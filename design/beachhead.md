# Spec — Beachheads & Bunkers (owner doctrine, forward design)

> Status: **forward design only — nothing is built.** This document captures the
> owner's beachhead doctrine as laid down live on **Oct 13 2026 (Hans)**. It is
> written to sit inside the already-locked battle doctrine (THE PLAYBOOK,
> Oct 4–5 2026; additions Oct 11 2026), the forward-base spec
> (`design/forward-base.md`), and the battle playbook / war-room formations
> work. Where this spec meets locked doctrine, locked doctrine wins unless
> Hans rules otherwise; everything he has not yet ruled on is listed under
> **§8 Open Questions** — do not invent answers to those.

---

## 0. What a beachhead is

A **beachhead** is the ground an invasion holds on someone else's world — the
landing front a cross-server force establishes and must keep, and the front a
defending world must hold against invaders. Cross-server invasions are
**Normandy-style chaos by design** (RAID / BATTLE DOCTRINE, Oct 4 2026) —
this spec is the ground-level shape of that chaos.

Hans, in his own words (Oct 13 2026):

> "A beachhead is miles long of an invasion."

The beachhead is not a skirmish point and not a flag. It is a **front** —
miles of contested shoreline that has to be manned along its length, or it
isn't held at all.

**Relationship to the Forward Base (FOB).** Per `forward-base.md` and the
RvR spec (§2/§12), a colony standing on enemy soil establishes a Forward
Base — the staging ground that receives and musters troops; lose it and you
are pushed off the zone. The beachhead is the **front around and beyond that
foothold**: the line of positions the landing force must fill and hold for
the FOB — and the invasion — to survive. (Exact formal relationship between
"beachhead held" and "FOB alive" is an open question, §8.)

## 1. The Wide Picture — the beachhead screen

Big battles are the design case: **30v30, 40v40, and beyond.** At that scale
nobody commands from the cockpit alone. (MEMORY.md: THE WIDE PICTURE,
Oct 13 2026 — battle design north star.)

- The beachhead is fought from a **big scroll-over screen**: the player pans
  across the **whole line** — miles of front — and reads it.
- The point of the wide view is judgment: **see the weak spots in the line**
  — your own thinning sectors and the enemy's — **reinforce your own** weak
  spots and **bust through theirs**.
- Commanding happens **from** the wide view: moves, reserves, cover orders —
  not just from the cockpit/first-person view. The radar / expanded map must
  grow into this screen; **a minimap toy does not satisfy it**.
- **Equal access (locked):** every player gets the **same** whole-beachhead
  view and the same ability to put troops where they're needed most. The
  wide picture is **not** a leader-only privilege.

---

## 2. Bunkers — presence is control

A beachhead is held through **bunkers**: fixed positions along the front.

- **Fixed positions.** Bunkers are placed by the beachhead's designed layout
  (§3) — they are ground truth on the map, not player-built improvisations
  (whether players can additionally *build* positions is open, §8).
- **Fill them and they hold.** Place troops in a bunker and those troops
  **HOLD** — in Hans's words, *"they just sit there, they're on hold."* A
  manned bunker is a held position.
- **Empty is empty.** A bunker or position with no troops in it is **EMPTY**
  ground. Empty ground can simply be occupied — *"the boys across the street
  can just move in and occupy."* No capture ritual makes an empty position
  yours; only bodies do.
- **The law of the beachhead:** you must **fortify the beachhead with
  troops** or it isn't yours. **Presence = control. Absence = an open
  door.**

This is the same commitment logic as the locked playbook — *casualties =
commitment*, *withdrawal requires relief* — applied to ground: holding a
beachhead is not a claim you stake once, it is a garrison you keep feeding.

## 3. Layout & scale — designed, and big

### 3.1 Designed, not scattered

A beachhead is **laid out** — a designed layout along the landing front, not
procedural scatter. Bunkers sit in **sectors/zones** along the miles-long
front: the landing strip where the force comes ashore, the sectors spreading
along the shore from it, and the depth behind them.

### 3.2 The numbers (Hans, Oct 13 2026 — corrected upward live)

Hans's first figure, then his correction, both in his words:

> *"At least 50 bunkers to be filled — that's half an alliance."*

> *"50 isn't even a drop — it should be about a thousand bunkers to be
> filled."*

So the spec records both, in the roles he gave them:

- **50 bunkers = the minimum** — the **first landing strip**: the smallest
  beachhead that counts as a beachhead at all. His population anchor: filling
  even this much is **half an alliance** (so a full alliance ≈ 100 players;
  holding one beachhead is a half-alliance commitment).
- **≈ 1,000 bunkers = the design target** for a full beachhead — sectors and
  zones along the whole front summing to roughly a thousand positions to be
  filled.

**Population math is an OPEN QUESTION (§8).** A thousand *filled* bunkers
implies a multi-alliance, server-scale war population. How that reconciles
with "50 = half an alliance" — whether positions are held by squads rather
than single players, whether alliances combine, whether most of the thousand
stand empty as the open doors both sides race for — is Hans's ruling to
give. This spec records the ambition and the tension; it does not resolve
it.

### 3.3 What the layout forms

How the sectors and bunkers are arranged — arcs, echelons, strongpoints,
depth lines, reserves behind the shore — is an **OPEN QUESTION (§8)**.
Locked doctrine the layout must respect either way:

- **Breach rule (Oct 11 2026):** intact Warden lines block ALL ground
  movement, including Reavers; only once enemy Wardens are ground down can
  Reavers (only Reavers) push through the breach. Bunker lines and Warden
  lines will interact — how is open (§8).
- **Rear entry (Oct 11 2026):** jump-ship combat drop ONLY, with ≥20%
  Warden escort. Ground movement can never get behind enemy lines — a
  bunker line cannot be walked around into the rear.
- **Siege (Oct 11 2026):** visible arcing shells, friendly fire is real, AoE
  falloff 2m:100% / 5m:70% / 10m:50% / 15m:30% / 20m:10% / 20m+:0 — bunker
  spacing and siege-versus-bunker behavior must be designed against these
  radii (details open, §8).

## 4. The dedicated garrison

Troops placed in beachhead bunkers are **DEDICATED to that beachhead** —
a committed garrison, not a passing assignment.

- A squad garrisoning a bunker is that beachhead's squad. It is not a unit
  that happens to be standing there between other jobs; manning the line
  *is* its job, for as long as it holds the position.
- This is the positional form of the locked maxims: **casualties =
  commitment** and **withdrawal requires relief** (THE PLAYBOOK, Oct 4–5
  2026; rearguard doctrine B10, RvR spec §15 — retreat is never free and
  never instant, and the covering force stays in the fight while the main
  body disengages).
- **What releases a garrison is an OPEN QUESTION (§8):** relief only (a
  fresh squad takes the bunker before the old one stands down)? A recall
  with a cost or a timer? Does an abandoned-then-empty bunker differ from a
  relieved one in any way beyond being an open door? Hans rules; until then,
  design assumption to test against his rulings: **relief is the clean
  path**, because it is the one the existing doctrine already blesses.

## 4A. Capture timing — taking a bunker is conversion work

**RULED (Hans, Oct 13 2026; duration provisional).** Capturing a bunker is
**not instant**, even after everyone defending it has been beaten. The
victorious force still has to convert the position before it is solidly
theirs:

1. clear the dead out of the bunker;
2. haul the enemy's shells and ammunition out;
3. stock their own ammunition and supplies in; and
4. prepare the position for their own use.

Hans's reasoning:

> "Just because you beat everybody in it doesn't mean that you can just go
> in and take it — there's a lot of bodies that have to be removed, there's
> shells to be moved, and things have to be prepared."

- A **conversion timer** runs from takeover until the bunker goes
  **solid** for its new holders. Winning the fight starts the conversion;
  it does not complete it.
- **The clock runs against the attacker, in the defender's favor.** Until
  the bunker goes solid, the defender may move back in at any time and keep
  restocking soldiers there. A converting bunker is therefore not yet
  secure ground — it remains a live fight for possession while the work is
  unfinished.
- **Provisional duration: ~30 minutes — tunable, NOT locked.** Hans
  floated 30 minutes while explicitly leaving the length undecided: *"I
  don't know if that's 30 minutes or what — we'll just have to kind of
  see."* Playtesting and later owner rulings set the real number.
- **Still open (§8, Q3):** whether defender re-entry resets the conversion
  timer or merely pauses it; whether partial restocking of the bunker
  counts toward conversion progress; and how the wide view shows a bunker
  that is converting / not yet solid.

## 5. The threat model — three planets at once

The scale in §3 exists because of the threat Hans named (Oct 13 2026):

> *"You might have three other planets invading you."*

- **Multi-planet assault is the design case — not 1v1.** A world can face
  **simultaneous invasions from several planets at once**, converging on it
  together.
- Beachhead sectors must therefore be defensible **under converging attack**:
  a front stripped to reinforce one landing is an open door at the next.
  This is precisely why the wide picture (§1) matters — the defender reads
  *all* the fronts at once and decides where the bodies go.
- **Live war display tie-in (locked, Oct 11 2026):** the portal/solar-system
  landing already shows live war — fires on a pounded planet, its reverb
  visibly dropping, and **war tracers**: shuttle trails and jump-ship
  penetration marks between planets, so converging invasions are *seen*
  converging. The beachhead war is the ground truth underneath that sky
  picture.
- **Defense tie-in (locked doctrine):** the Logistics Hall playbook —
  pre-made response loadouts and pre-formed response plans with preset
  muster timers, auto mode running the plan when nobody is driving — is the
  machinery a world answers a multi-planet klaxon with. Beachhead defense
  sectors and playbook response plans must be designed together.
- **How many simultaneous beachheads/fronts one world can be forced to hold
  is an OPEN QUESTION (§8).**

## 6. How the Chorus holds ground (defender AI)

Where the defender is the Chorus rather than players, its beachhead holding
follows the locked CHORUS DOCTRINE + DEFENSE GRADES (Oct 6 2026): the Chorus
fights like a survivor, not a conqueror — **layered defense first** (tanks
forward, ranged behind, pre-sighted siege, deep reserve), counterattacking
when the attacker bleeds (~60% strength or a broken vector). The five graded
templates (G1 Screen 0.6× → G2 Picket 0.8× → G3 Bulwark 1.0× → G4 Fortress
1.25× → G5 Bastion 1.5×) are the starting vocabulary for how full and how
layered an AI-manned bunker line is at each grade. Mapping grades onto
bunker sectors is design work under this spec, not new doctrine.

---

## 7. Fallen heroes on the beachhead

**Fallen Hero Recovery (locked, Oct 12 2026)** applies on beachhead ground
as on any field: a fallen hero's gear drops where they fell; an extraction
crew can rescue (anyone claims first — body and gear are separate rolls, the
body may return without the gear); there is a **5-minute window**, after
which the loot goes 100% to the other side; recovery takes **24h** (the
store's Recovery Expedite exists). A miles-long front means fallen heroes
can fall far from help — extraction across a contested beachhead, including
past empty (enemy-occupiable) bunkers, is a designed risk of the wide front,
not an edge case.

## 8. Open questions — Hans's rulings needed

1. **Bunker capacity:** how many troops/squads fill one bunker? Does
   capacity vary by position (shore strongpoint vs. rear position)?
2. **Fortification:** does "fortify" mean only manning pre-placed bunkers,
   or can positions be built/upgraded? If built: cost, build time, and can
   building happen under fire?
3. **Occupation / capture time — RULED (Oct 13 2026), duration
   provisional:** capturing a defended bunker is **not instant** after its
   defenders are beaten. A conversion timer runs from takeover until the
   bunker is solidly the takers' — the dead must be cleared, enemy
   shells/ammo hauled out, and the takers' own ammunition and supplies
   stocked in (see §4A). Until it goes solid, the defender may move back
   in at any time and keep restocking soldiers; the clock runs against the
   attacker. **Duration: ~30 minutes, provisional/tunable and not locked**
   — Hans has not settled the final length. Still open: does defender
   re-entry reset the timer or pause it; does partial restocking count
   toward conversion; and how does the wide view show a converting /
   not-yet-solid bunker?
4. **"Hold" fire behavior:** what does the HOLD stance mean for fire —
   hold fire until fired on, engage anything in range/arc, pre-sighted
   fields of fire only? Do held troops ever pursue?
5. **Siege vs. bunkers:** how do bunker positions interact with siege AoE
   (the locked falloff radii) — do bunkers grant cover/reduction, can a
   bunker be destroyed and the position become open ground?
6. **Bunkers vs. the breach rule:** does a manned bunker line count as a
   "line" for movement-blocking purposes like Warden lines, or do bunkers
   only control their own ground while the Warden breach rule governs
   movement between them?
7. **Garrison release:** what frees a dedicated garrison — relief only, a
   recall with cost/timer, rotation schedules? What does abandoning a
   bunker (leaving it empty) cost beyond the open door itself?
8. **Population math:** ≈1,000 filled bunkers vs. "50 = half an alliance" —
   how does Hans want this reconciled (squad-held positions, combined
   alliances, most positions standing empty as contested doors)?
9. **Layout geometry:** what arcs/sectors/depth structure do the ~1,000
   positions form — echelons, strongpoint chains, a shore line with
   depth lines behind it?
10. **Simultaneous fronts:** how many beachheads can one world be forced to
    hold at once (the "three planets invading" case and beyond) — is there
    a cap, and what happens at it?
11. **Wide-view representation:** how does the wide picture show bunker
    control state at a glance — manned/empty/contested/under-fire — so a
    player can read a miles-long line's weak spots in seconds?
12. **Beachhead vs. FOB:** what is the formal win/loss relationship — does
    losing all bunkers kill the forward base, does the FOB's fall empty the
    beachhead, or are they scored separately?
13. **Attacker-side bunkers:** are bunkers defender positions only, or does
    the landing force also dig into bunker positions as it expands the
    beachhead inland?
